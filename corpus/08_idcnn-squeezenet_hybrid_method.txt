IDCNN–SqueezeNet Hybrid Method

The conference paper, “IDCNN-SqueezeNet Architecture for Depression Detection from Social Media Data,” proposes a hybrid model for depression detection from Twitter text. Its four preprocessing operations are removal of punctuation, removal of URLs, removal of stop words, and tokenization. The engineered features are TF-IDF, n-grams, and Bag of Words (BoW). These features pass into a classifier that combines an Improved Deep Convolutional Neural Network (IDCNN) with SqueezeNet.

The proposed IDCNN is described as including three convolutional layers, two max-pooling layers, and one average-pooling layer. It also introduces three custom Local Response Normalization (LRN) layers after convolutional operations. ReLU activations, dropout, flattening, and fully connected layers appear in the described computation. Custom LRN is intended to balance activation responses and make the representation more robust.

SqueezeNet is the second model component. The conference paper describes its use of compact 1-by-1 convolutions and Fire modules to reduce parameter needs. It identifies eight Fire modules and a sequence extending from conv1 to conv10, with dropout and pooling. SqueezeNet is chosen partly for architectural efficiency and relatively limited processing demands. Rather than declaring one model a preprocessing step for the other, the paper presents two models whose resulting scores or outputs are averaged to obtain the final result.

The hybrid is denoted IHC (IDCNN + SqueezeNet) in the experimental discussion. Its role is different from the SASSOA and DU-POA optimizers in the journal articles. IDCNN and SqueezeNet are predictive network components, while SASSOA and DU-POA are schemes for training/weight optimization elsewhere in this research set.

A question-answering model should be able to retrieve the IDCNN layer arrangement, the three kinds of extracted features, the reason for using SqueezeNet, and the score averaging method without confusing the conference paper with either of the journal studies.

Source coverage: Paper C, Sections 3–5.
