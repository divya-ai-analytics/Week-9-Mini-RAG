Depression and Suicide Features and Fusion

The second journal article, “Dwarf Updated Pelican Optimization Algorithm for Depression and Suicide Detection from Social Media,” proposes the SADDSM pipeline. It first preprocesses the collected text using stop-word removal, tokenization, and stemming. The next step generates three types of information: Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF), improved Word2Vec, and style features.

TF-IDF describes term importance by combining within-document frequency with a penalty for terms common across a document collection. Improved Word2Vec is included to capture word associations and semantic relationships that are not apparent from a simple word-frequency inventory. Style features provide another representation of writing patterns. Their inclusion reflects the paper’s aim of capturing both semantic and writing-based cues in the observed posts.

The article then proposes modified feature-level fusion based on a modified mutual information score. The stated motivation is that retaining separate, high-dimensional feature representations can complicate classification. The fusion stage selects or combines informative features before they enter the detection ensemble. The research thus explicitly separates feature extraction from feature-level fusion.

The classification stage uses an ensemble of Deep Belief Network (DBN), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), and improved Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM). The corresponding optimization scheme, DU-POA, adjusts RNN and improved LSTM weights. It is not a replacement for TF-IDF, Word2Vec, or the fusion method; each element has a distinct position in the pipeline.

When using this text as RAG reference material, the answer to “What is modified mutual information used for?” is feature-level fusion, while the answer to “Which optimizer tunes the classifiers?” is Dwarf Updated Pelican Optimization Algorithm. Those roles should not be conflated. Paper B reports performance improvements for the complete method and in ablation comparisons; the study should not be interpreted as proving that any isolated feature is sufficient for diagnosis.
